# The scene

One drawing, three layers and an action, composed into one document and shown at
`/station/`. Everything here is vector, generated by a script, deterministic: the
same seed and the same bytes in, the same bytes out.

## The canvas

`viewBox="0 0 2400 1500"`. A layer's root carries no `width` or `height`, no
background and no filters; it has its own `<title>` and `role="img"`, so it stands
alone in a browser.

| Zone | Extent | Who draws it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Station | x 560–1840, y 60–900, drawn 96 units right of centre | `assets/scene/station.svg` |
| Traffic | x 0–2400, y 120–1240, behind and in front of the station | `assets/scene/traffic.svg` |
| City | x 0–2400, ground plane y 860–1500 | `assets/scene/city.svg` |

The palette is the site's, by name and by hex, because an SVG that stands alone
cannot read a stylesheet:

| Token | Hex | Use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `--amber` | `#ffc24a` | the signal: carrier lines, the one alert lamp, live fittings |
| `--state` | `#8fd3ff` | state: docking geometry, alignment beam, the open bay |
| `--cream` | `#efe6d3` | structure, and the top of the value ramp |
| `--dust` | `#a79085` | secondary structure, haze, tracks |
| `--alert` | `#e2402f` | alert, sparingly — 9 marks in the whole composition |
| `--void` | `#000000` | the ground |

Line weights are 1, 2 and 4 only. Depth comes from value and weight, not hue.

## The light, the values, the occlusion rule

Three rules do most of the work of making the frame read as a drawing of objects
rather than as a wireframe of a machine. Every layer obeys them, and the
measurement in `scripts/scene/measure.py` reads them back off the written file.

**One light.** `LIGHT = (0.46, 0.78, 0.43)` in the station's own frame: high,
from the left of the drawing, a little towards the viewer. Every stroke in
`station.py` carries the normal of the face it belongs to, and the lit side of a
member is drawn heavier than its shadow side at every depth. The direction is a
constant, not a per-seed draw, and the city and traffic layers are lit from the
same quarter — the city's two visible faces are its front (unlit at night) and
the side that faces the vanishing point, which is the lit one.

The light is expressed as **weight**, and depth as **value**. A lit near member
is 4 or 2; its shadow side is 1 or 2. That way a face can be both far and lit
without the two rules fighting.

**Three values.** Near structure is cream `#efe6d3`, the middle distance is dust
`#a79085`, the far field is dust at half opacity. The cut depths are ink
quantiles, not fixed fractions of the bounding box: a third of the station's ink
is the near role, about half is the middle distance, and the rest is the far
field, whatever proportions the seed draws. Pure light — cream at weight 4 — is
reserved for the nearest primary silhouette and measures 3.8 % of the station's
ink, and under 5 % of the composed frame.

Per layer, measured on the committed files by `scripts/scene/measure.py`:

| Layer | near | mid | far | accent | steps |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| station | 29.6 % | 52.1 % | 14.9 % | 3.3 % | cream 4/2/1, dust 2/1, dust@0.5 |
| city | 26.4 % | 56.1 % | 16.5 % | 0.9 % | cream 2, dust 2/1, dust@0.55 |
| traffic | 21.3 % | 7.7 % | 68.3 % | 2.6 % | cream 2, dust 2/1 at 0.5–0.9 |
| composed | 25.2 % | 43.8 % | 28.7 % | 2.3 % | — |

**Occlusion.** A line that passes behind a nearer form and continues through it
reads as wireframe, so every opaque form registers a solid in the station's
frame — a cylinder for a module or a collar's barrel, a rectangle for a solar
wing or a radiator, an annulus for a habitat ring's rim band, a disc for a dish,
a rectangle for the hanging truss section. After the sketch is built, every
stroke is walked in screen steps of 2.5 units and tested against the solids
nearer than it; where a nearer form covers it the stroke is cut, and the cut is
bisected to land a twentieth of a unit short of the occluding edge, on the
visible side. A form never occludes its own strokes, so a module keeps its own
far ribs and loses the truss behind it.

Measured by `scripts/scene/check_occlusion.py`, which rebuilds the geometry and
asks the question independently of the clipper:

| | hidden samples | violations |
| --- | --- | --- |
| before (seed 1, `bias 0`) | 2 809 | 1 543 |
| after (seed 1, `bias 96`) | 2 809 | 84 collinear, 12 by ink class |

The 84 are samples where two different solids' ink is collinear within half a
unit — a 1 px crossing, not a visible line through a form. The city and the
traffic have their own occlusion: the city fills nearer blocks black so they
cover the rows behind them.

## The composition

Nothing important is centred. The station is drawn 96 canvas units right of the
middle (`BIAS_X` in `station.py`), so the frame's left third is open sky; the
gantry stands over the open bay rather than on the centre line; and its mast is
solved against the projected frame so that its top leaves the frame at y −40 for
any seed. One element leaves the frame, the mass is off-axis, and the mirror is
broken by the four solar wings, which are never drawn at equal spans.

The detail that gives the drawing its scale is repeated at a stated pitch, and
the pitch is a parameter rather than a coincidence:

| Pitch | Where | Value (seed 1) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Truss rungs | one third of a bay, on both bottom chords | 37.7 units |
| Gantry ties | up both legs, and the lattice's diagonals | 26.0 units |
| Antenna rungs | along every dish mast | 13.0 units |
| Collar bolts | around every collar | 6 per collar |
| Ring seams | around every habitat ring, alternating with lit windows | 15° |
| Panel pitch | every solar panel and every fourth rail | 18.2 units |
| City floors / windows | world units, so they shrink with depth | 2.6 / 1.9 world |

## Station — `scripts/scene/station.py` → `assets/scene/station.svg`

```
python scripts/scene/station.py --seed 1 --out assets/scene/station.svg
python scripts/scene/station.py --report      # occlusion, pitches, mast height
```

The station is a single three-dimensional sketch — X along the truss spine, Y up,
Z towards the viewer — projected once through a pinhole camera whose principal
point is the scene's vanishing point on the horizon (1200, 980), then fitted into
the station zone. Nothing is drawn in 2D directly: rings are circles in the Y–Z
plane, modules are faceted tubes, arrays are panel quads on a boom, and the
perspective, the ellipse shapes and the left-to-right size falloff all fall out
of the projection. The primitive set is small and every primitive takes a handful
of parameters: **truss bay** (bay length, truss radius, one diagonal per face),
**faceted tube** (axis, length, radius, rib positions, eight longitudinal edges),
**collar** (ring, inner ring, six capture bolts, cross), **adapter cone**,
**habitat ring** (radius, spokes, rim thickness, plating seams, alternating lit
windows), **panel wing** (row count, column count, pitch, deployment fraction),
**radiator** (length, width, fin count, mounting angle), **antenna** (mast rungs,
struts, dish), **gantry** (leg spread, height, lattice beam, ties, a mast with a
windlass that leaves the frame), **cable** (sagging polyline), and the
**suspended truss section** on the hook.

Construction is structural rather than decorative — the near face of one bay is
missing with amber caps on the cut longerons, a gantry straddles that bay with a
section hanging from its hook on a red-tagged block, two cyan guide lines and a
cyan socket collar mark that bay as the live one, and the lower array on the near
mast stops part-deployed with its boom continuing as a dashed line to a clamped
tip. Accents are only where something functional happens: the cut caps, the one
alert tag, the docking socket, and the windlass lamp. The ring windows, the
collar rings and the wing rails are structure and are drawn in the structure's
own ink.

| Parameter | Range across seeds | Drives |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `bays` | 10–14 | spine length = bays × bay length |
| `bay_len` | 96–126 | truss panel size, and the rung pitch (a third of it) |
| `truss_r` | 42–58 | truss depth, and the size of every module collar |
| `ring_count` | 1–3 | habitat rings on the spine |
| `ring_r` | 150–210, capped at 0.16 L for three rings | ring diameter and spoke length |
| `ring_spokes` | 8–12 | spokes per ring |
| `wing_rows` | 11–16 | rows of panels per array |
| `panel_cols` | 3–5 | panels across an array |
| `panel_pitch` | 16–34 (derived: 0.225–0.265 L ÷ rows) | panel size, and so the array span |
| `module_len` | 96–152 | axial and radial module length |
| `module_r` | 46–74 | module diameter |
| `radiator_count` | 1–2 | radiator fins near the centreline |
| `antenna_count` | 1–2 | dish masts on the spine |
| `open_bay` | middle bay ± 1 | which bay is under construction |
| `yaw` | 17°–27° | truss foreshortening and ring ellipse ratio |
| `roll` | −3°–+3° | spine tilt inside the zone |
| `deploy` | 0.42–0.72 | how much of the lower array is deployed |

Two of those are derived rather than sampled, so that a seed changes the shape
without changing the envelope: the panel pitch is chosen from the wing span the
seed asks for, and the ring radius is capped by the spine length so three rings
stay clear of the open bay. The fit is solved per seed by bisection on camera
distance (to the zone's width or height, whichever binds) and on camera height
(to centre the drawing vertically), so any seed lands inside the zone with the
horizon still at y = 980; the composition bias and the mast are applied after
that fit, so a seed's scale never depends on how far the crop reaches.

`--seed N` anywhere in 0…2³² renders a different station; the four proofs shipped
with the layer are seeds 1, 6, 11 and 26. `--check` re-parses the written file
and fails on the layer contract: viewBox, no width/height, a title and
`role="img"`, palette-only strokes, weights in {1, 2, 4}, no `filter`/`href`,
under 150 KB, and byte-identical to a fresh render of the same seed.

## Traffic — `scripts/scene/traffic.py` → `assets/scene/traffic.svg`

```
python scripts/scene/traffic.py --seed 1977 --out assets/scene/traffic.svg
```

Two groups, because the compositor puts this layer on both sides of the station
and the city: `#traffic-back` (the far sky, y 120–1020) and `#traffic-front` (the
near sky, y 1080–1500, cropped by the frame). The far band is drawn at 0.62
stroke-opacity on its group, so the haze thins it by depth rather than washing
over the picture.

The primitive set is four craft families on one path primitive. `orbit` is an arc
through three canvas points, or a closed loop: the points are squashed by an axis
ratio, a circle is fitted through the squashed points, and the result is squashed
back into an ellipse, so a path can be placed where it reads instead of wherever
an ellipse happens to fall. Every orbit is drawn twice: as a dotted or dashed
trail in dust (the path owns the read), and as the sockets its craft ride, rolled
to the path tangent. Dots are 2 px on, `--dens` px off, weight 1; the foreground
trails are 20/36 dashes at weight 2. `inspector` is a square hull with two booms,
9–14 px wide in the sky and 2.6× that in the foreground band. `relay` is a pair
of 2 px diamonds on a link with a cyan sync tick. `bus` is a nine-point hull
polygon carrying two solar arrays, one function from a 44 px craft in the sky to
the 980 px crop. That crop is the fifth primitive: a `bus` at 980 × 78 px with a
black ground fill so it occludes whatever is behind it, a 25 px cyan beacon, and
a 520 px amber plume, entering low and left and leaving through the bottom-right.
`tether` is two bodies on one 500–550 px line. `alert` is the layer's only red: a
26 px reticle with four ticks and a dashed drift line, upper right.

The seeding controls the things that should vary between frames and nothing else:
`--constellations` (5–6 sky constellations carrying craft), `--dens` (dot gap
4–8 px), `--angle` (the close craft's roll, 19–28°), and, inside each
constellation, its radii by ±10 %, its rotation by ±7°, its span by ±6° and its
craft count by one either way. An arc's span is trimmed to its longest run inside
the frame band, so no seed can push a loop past the top edge. `--stats` prints each
path's radii, span, x and y extent and craft count: across seeds 1977, 1042, 3301
and 8842 the far sky holds 0.9–1.2 % of its band in ink, the station's airspace
0.5–0.7 %, and the foreground band 8.3–8.7 %, with nothing drawn above y 120.

## City and ground — `scripts/scene/city.py` → `assets/scene/city.svg`

```
python scripts/scene/city.py --seed 1981 --out assets/scene/city.svg
```

One pinhole camera, `CAM_H` 55 world units above the plane, `FOCAL` 900 px,
vanishing point at the centre of the horizon, so every edge in the drawing is a
vertical, a horizontal, or a line through that point. The city is kept out of a
corridor where `|x| = 0.42 · z`, which projects to a fixed ±378 px from the
vanishing point: the middle of the frame stays open ground for the station and
the traffic, and the massing closes in on both sides.

Depth is carried by value and line weight, never hue: the near tier is 2 px
cream, the middle distance 2 px dust, the far tier 1 px dust at 0.55 opacity, and
black fills make a nearer mass occlude what stands behind it. Windows are value,
not accent — one dashed row per storey in the tier's own ink, with a seeded 1 in
8 left amber, so the lights in the city read as somebody's rather than as a
pattern. Cyan marks two antennae; red marks two beacons on the tallest towers and
nothing else; the foreground rail is the only 4 px cream in the layer, because it
is the closest thing in the frame.

**Primitives, all in world coordinates unless noted:**

- `block(xs0, xs1, z0, z1, top_y)` — the unit of the city. A front face at `z0`,
  a roof running back to `z1`, and the one side face that faces the vanishing
  point, black-filled and stroked on its silhouette and its corners. `simple`
  drops to the four lines a 1 px far block can carry.
- `window_rows(...)` — one dashed row per storey at the tier's value, the dash
  pattern jittered per block so no two facades light alike; floor height 2.6 and
  window pitch 1.9 world units, so the marks shrink with depth and disappear past
  the point where they would be smaller than a pixel.
- `roof_detail(...)` — a parapet plus one of a mast with crossbars, a plant room,
  a dish, or a water tank.
- `pad(...)` — the assembly hardstand: outline, inner edge, radial and cross
  joints, a tie-down grid, a pad circle with crosshair, corner marks, and hazard
  ticks along the front edge.

## The composition, mechanically

`scripts/scene/compose.py` reads the three layers, keeps their z-order and writes
`assets/art/station.svg` plus the same bytes at `_includes/scene/station.svg`,
which `station.html` includes inline so the groups are in the page's DOM for CSS
and for a script. The two paths are written in one invocation, so they cannot
drift:

```
python scripts/scene/compose.py              # both files, seed 20261127
python scripts/scene/compose.py --check      # exit 1 if either is stale
python scripts/scene/compose.py --layers DIR # compose from a directory of layers
python scripts/scene/compose.py --out FILE   # one path, for a quick look
```

Bands, in document order, every one present in every build:

| Group | Contents |
| --- | --- |
| `#scene-ground` | the void rectangle the whole canvas sits on |
| `#scene-city` | the city layer |
| `#scene-traffic-back` | the traffic layer's far band (`#traffic-back`) |
| `#scene-haze` | the dust pass — generated here, 1 px, behind the station |
| `#scene-station` | the station layer, and inside it `#station-body`, `-rings`, `-wings`, `-masts`, `-mast`, `-gantry`, `-hook`, `-rungs`, `-seams`, `-bolts`, `-radiators`, `-modules` |
| `#scene-traffic-front` | the traffic layer's near band (`#traffic-front`) |
| `#scene-action` | the action — generated here |

The compositor is also where a layer's ids are made safe: each is prefixed with
the layer's name (`city-ground`, `station-rings`), and every `url(#…)` and
`href="#…"` that pointed at one is rewritten with it. The ids above, and the
action's own (`#docking-shuttle`, `#alignment-beam`, `#alignment-line`,
`#dock-arm`, `#dock-mark`, `#signal-carrier`, `#signal-pulse`, `#drone-a`,
`#drone-b`), are reserved and never prefixed. A layer that is missing is drawn as
a placeholder frame with its own name in it, and the header comment of the
composed file says so.

## The action

The action is placed from the station *zone* in the contract, not from a
measurement of the station drawing, so the truss, the hoop and the approach line
land in the same place whatever seed drew the station.

| Primitive | Parameter | Value |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Truss line | y, x range, as fractions of the zone | 0.58 of the height → y 547.2; 0.05–0.88 of the width → x 624–1686 |
| Docking hoop | centre, as fractions of the zone | (0.95, 0.74) → (1776, 681.6) |
| Boom | hull anchor → hoop | (0.84, 0.70) → (1635.2, 648) to the hoop |
| Shuttle | centre, as fractions of the zone | (0.40, 0.86) → (1072, 782.4), clamped above y 836 |
| Alignment beam | cone, gates, centreline | edges ±24 at the hoop; gates at 0.34, 0.55, 0.74 of the beam with half-lengths 27, 20, 14; a 2 px centreline dashed `12 8` |
| Drones | two cubic arcs, phase, size | `M-90 690 C760 556 1560 512 2490 640` at 0.34, 13 units; `M2490 300 C1720 236 880 372 -90 306` at 0.62, 10 units |
| Pulse | carrier, dot, tails | 1 px amber carrier on a 96-unit tick pitch; a 4 px amber dot, a 2 px tail of 38 units and a 1 px tail of 66 |
| Haze | count, length, opacity, dash | 34 runs of 120–760 units at 0.07–0.17, 42 % of them dashed `2 7`; 5 curves at 0.12 |

The haze, the ticks and the pulse's starting point are the only seeded parts of
the action; everything else is a fraction of the zone, so a new seed moves the
atmosphere and leaves the staging alone.

## Motion

`assets/art/station.svg` is a still. `station.html` moves it in CSS, with no
script, by naming the composed document's groups. Each handle is a bare group —
the transform that places the thing sits on a child — so the handle's own
transform belongs to the animation, and to the next thing that drives it.

| Handle | Property | Duration, curve |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `#scene-city`, `#scene-traffic-back` | `transform` — 9 units | 120 s, ease-in-out, alternate |
| `#scene-station` | `transform` — 15 units and a 6-unit lift | 96 s, ditto |
| `#scene-traffic-front` | `transform` — 30 units and a 6-unit lift | 72 s, ditto |
| `#signal-pulse` | `transform`, `opacity` — 1062 units, the truss | 16 s, linear |
| `#alignment-line` | `stroke-dashoffset` — 20 units | 6 s, linear |
| `#docking-shuttle` | `transform` — 26 units along the approach | 20 s, ease-in-out, alternate |
| `#drone-a`, `#drone-b` | `transform` — 26 and 22 units | 44 s, ease-in-out, alternate |
| `#station-masts` | `transform` — 2.2 units against the hull's drift, 0.9 lift | 11 s, ease-in-out, alternate, −3.1 s |
| `#station-mast` | `transform` — 2.2 units, its own period | 17 s, ditto, −1.7 s |
| `#station-hook` | `transform` — rotate ±0.6° about the hook | 7.5 s, ditto, −2.6 s |

Seven animated properties in eight keyframe sets, on fourteen elements — twelve
in all when the page is read. The three sway handles are the secondary motion:
the dish masts lag the hull and lean the other way from it, the gantry's tower
moves at its own period, and the hanging truss section swings on the hook that
carries it. Three periods and three negative delays, so no two parts of the
station are at the top of their swing together and nothing in the frame moves as
one rigid piece.

The frame clips, so the drift can never show an edge. `prefers-reduced-motion:
reduce` stops all of it and hides the pause switch; the *Hold the scene* switch
pauses it without leaving the page, which is the WCAG 2.2.2 answer for motion that
runs for minutes. Nothing on the page depends on the animation to be visible or
readable: stopped, the drawing is exactly where the file put it.

## Measuring the drawing

Two scripts stand in for eyes, because the crew that draws these files cannot see
them:

```
python scripts/scene/measure.py assets/art/station.svg --json after.json
python scripts/scene/check_occlusion.py assets/scene/station.svg --seed 1
```

`measure.py` reports ink length and weight, the ink share in each value role, the
value steps in use, a 4×4 density grid with its empty cells and its dense-to-sparse
ratio, detected detail pitches (modal spacing between parallel neighbours, in any
class), and an ASCII map of the frame at 96 columns. `check_occlusion.py` counts
hidden-line violations against the geometry, independently of the clipper, and
exits 1 when it finds one.
