# The station scene

One vector scene, built as three layers that the compositor merges into
`assets/art/station.svg`. The canvas is `viewBox="0 0 2400 1500"` and every layer is
a standalone SVG with its own `<title>`, `role="img"`, no root `width` or `height`, a
transparent background, and no external references. Each layer is generated by a
stdlib script under `scripts/scene/` and is byte-identical on a re-run with the same
seed:

```
python scripts/scene/traffic.py --seed 1977 --out assets/scene/traffic.svg
```

Shared rules: stroked geometry and flat shapes; line weights 1, 2 and 4 only;
depth by value and line weight, never by hue; one named scrim and no other gradient;
no filters and no raster. The palette is the site's tokens only — amber `#ffc24a`
for signal, cyan `#8fd3ff` for state, cream `#efe6d3` for structure, dust `#a79085`
for secondary structure, red `#e2402f` for the one alert, and the ground `#000000`.
The layers are drawn on the black ground, so they are read against `--void`; on a
white page the cream loses its value and the layer is not meant to be looked at there.

## traffic

`scripts/scene/traffic.py`, `assets/scene/traffic.svg`, 12–14 KB. Two groups, because
the compositor puts this layer on both sides of the station and the city:
`#traffic-back` (the far sky, y 120–1020) and `#traffic-front` (the near sky,
y 1080–1500, cropped by the frame).

The primitive set is four craft families on one path primitive.

`orbit` is an arc through three canvas points, or a closed loop. The three points are
squashed by an axis ratio (the ellipse's minor-to-major ratio, seeded around the
value in the table), a circle is fitted through the squashed points, and the result is
squashed back into an ellipse; the parametric angles of the three points then give the
span to draw exactly. That means a path can be placed where it reads — the two side
thirds and the band behind the station — instead of wherever an ellipse happens to
fall, and the curvature of each arc is a number I can measure. Every orbit is drawn
twice: as a dotted or dashed trail in dust (the path owns the read), and as the
sockets its craft ride, rolled to the path tangent. Dots are 2 px on, `--dens` px off,
weight 1; the foreground trails are 20/36 dashes at weight 2.

`inspector` is a square hull with two booms, 9–14 px wide in the sky and 2.6× that in
the foreground band, solid cream below about 6 px and outlined above it. `relay` is a
pair of 2 px diamonds on a link with a cyan sync tick, 25–30 px end to end, sent in
threes and fours. `bus` is a nine-point hull polygon carrying two solar arrays, with
the array outline, the boom lines and — for the large hulls — five cell dividers on
each array: one function from a 44 px craft in the sky to the 980 px crop.

That crop is the fifth primitive: `close` is a `bus` at 980 × 78 px with a black
ground fill so it occludes whatever is behind it, a 25 px cyan beacon, and a 520 px
amber `plume` cone on the engine, placed at the local origin so the engine can be
aimed. It enters low and slightly left, takes a seeded roll of 19–28°, leaves the
frame through the bottom-right, and the plume streaks back up the frame. `tether` is
two bodies on one 500–550 px line, riding its own short foreground path, tilted as a
single object. `alert` is the layer's only red: a 26 px reticle with four ticks, a
dashed drift line and three shrinking dots, in the upper right at
x 1980–2090, y 430–500, clear of the station's column.

The seeding controls the things that should vary between frames and nothing else:
`--constellations` (5–6 sky constellations carrying craft; the rest of the template
table flies as bare paths), `--dens` (dot gap 4–8 px), `--angle` (the close craft's
roll, 19–28°), and, inside each constellation, its radii by ±10%, its rotation by ±7°,
its span by ±6° and its craft count by one either way. The three design points of each
arc move by ±14 px. Everything else — the families, the sizes, the palette, the band
each element belongs to — is fixed, so a different seed is a different sky, not a
different drawing.

Two checks are built into the script rather than assumed. An arc's span is trimmed to
its longest run inside the frame band, so no seed can push a loop past the top edge;
and the far-focus arcs (the domes and the foreground bowl) carry their centre along
with their radius jitter, so their visible extreme stays put. `--stats` prints each
path's radii, span, x and y extent and craft count, which is how the band contract in
this document was measured: across seeds 1977, 1042, 3301 and 8842 the far sky holds
0.9–1.2% of its band in ink, the station's airspace 0.5–0.7%, and the foreground band
8.3–8.7%, with nothing drawn above y 120.
