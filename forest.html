The forest

A bear of three hundred and sixty-eight triangles walks a low-poly forest, and every repository is a parcel in it: a box with a ribbon and a tag, standing under its own fir, holding a name, a sentence, a link and the plant it grows on the garden page. There are nineteen and there is no map of them — the bear hops a station at a time, and the parcel at each one is in the undergrowth rather than on a shelf. Every triangle is arithmetic in seven files of JavaScript: no model file, no image, no library but the renderer.

    The bear is made in code: a body, a head of eighty faces, two ears on their own pivots, a muzzle, a nose, two eyes that blink on their own clock, four limbs with paw pads, a ruff, a belly of tufts and a tail, each one a primitive pushed through assets/forest/mesh.js. A parcel is eighty-two triangles — a box, a lid on a hinge, two ribbons, a bow, a tag on its own pivot — and it is placed by a seed, on the ground, at the foot of a fir, on a side the seed chooses. Opened, the ribbon slackens and drops, the lid overshoots, the paper folds flat, and the repository's plant grows out of the box; the sentence, the link and the specimen arrive in the panel beside the picture. Opening one is a button, or the parcel itself; it stays open in this browser, which is the only thing the page stores. Under prefers-reduced-motion: reduce he stands still, holds a pose, and the sentences arrive at once.
